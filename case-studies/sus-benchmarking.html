So What

Higher Logic was making consequential product decisions — consolidate two legacy marketing platforms and modernize its flagship community product before expanding AI into all products — with no usability data to inform either. As the company's first UX Researcher, I built the measurement infrastructure from nothing: three studies, three products, 227 participants, a mixed-methods framework that paired defensible scores with qualitative depth, and a research-to-operations pipeline that connected findings directly to at-risk customer accounts. The result was a shared language for quality that didn't exist before I got there.

Background

The Problem: No Shared Language for Quality

When I joined Higher Logic in 2021 as the company's first-ever UX Researcher, the product team faced a strategic challenge with no data to answer it: before investing in modernizing or consolidating its portfolio, leadership needed to understand where each product actually stood with users.

Higher Logic had three core products: Real Magnet and Informz — two legacy marketing automation platforms that needed honest evaluation before any consolidation decisions — and Higher Logic Thrive, the flagship online community platform being primed for AI feature expansion. The MA products needed assessment to understand what was salvageable. Thrive needed a current-state baseline before new complexity got layered on top.

Quality conversations were driven by whoever complained loudest: support tickets, CSM escalations, sales call anecdotes. There was no consistent measurement and no way to separate signal from noise. SUS studies had never been run at Higher Logic. My mandate was to change that.

Research Design

The Approach: SUS + Qualitative Context

I used the System Usability Scale (SUS) — a validated 10-item Likert questionnaire — as the quantitative backbone of each study. SUS produces a 0–100 score mapped to an industry-standard adjective scale, making it ideal for benchmarking and competitive comparison across products.

Paired with five open-ended qualitative questions and thematic coding of all written responses, the mixed-methods design gave leadership both a defensible number and a rich explanation of what was driving it — in the same deliverable.

Five Qualitative Questions

  • Q1

    Rating Explanation

    Please explain why you ranked the questions the way you did.

  • Q2

    Frustrations

    What, if anything, is currently frustrating for you in [product]?

  • Q3

    Missing Features

    What functionality is currently missing?

  • Q4

    What's Working

    Please describe anything working well that we should not change.

  • Q5

    Anything Else

    Please share anything else about your experience that you want us to know.

Execution

Three Studies, Three Products, Three and a Half Years

Each study was deployed sequentially, with recruitment and strategic framing tailored to a specific business question. Surveys distributed via SurveyMonkey; responses coded in structured spreadsheets and synthesized into executive decks with supporting data appendices.

  1. Dec 2021

    Real Magnet

    • 58 participants

    Higher Logic's oldest marketing automation product, built in the mid-2000s and deeply embedded in long-tenured customer workflows. The strategic question: what's salvageable, and what's broken beyond repair? Recruited via direct customer outreach. When scores came back below benchmark, leadership wasn't surprised — the technical debt was well known. What they'd never had was a prioritized, structured articulation of exactly where the problems lived.

  2. Oct 2022

    Informz

    • 107 participants

    The more actively used of the two marketing automation platforms. Strategic question: how does it compare to Real Magnet, and what would it take to modernize it into a combined offering? Expanded recruitment to three channels: direct email, CSM touchpoints, and a Pendo in-app prompt — yielding the largest sample of the three studies. When scores came back below benchmark, leadership was more surprised than they'd been with RM. Collaborated with my manager to identify quick-win improvements for the dev team to ship immediately, then closed the feedback loop by communicating those changes back to participants — building a reusable research panel in the process.

  3. May 2025

    Higher Logic Thrive

    • 73 participants · 88% qual response rate

    By 2025, Thrive had become the centerpiece of Higher Logic's strategy, and leadership was preparing to accelerate AI feature development across the platform. Strategic question: before integrating AI and consolidating products into Thrive, what do customers actually think of it right now? Recruited through Pendo in-app (39) and a Higher Logic User Group community post (34), generating 193 total qualitative comments from 64 respondents. Full report delivered to my product manager in June 2025.

Deep Dive — Thrive Study, May 2025

What the Data Revealed

Qualitative coding surfaced four dominant themes cutting across admins, marketers, end users, and implementers — suggesting systemic rather than role-specific friction. 44 of 73 participants provided written responses, generating patterns consistent enough to drive prioritized recommendations.

Theme 1 — Reporting Is a Major Bottleneck

The most frequently surfaced concern across both low and mid scorers. Users described split views between message-level and platform-level analytics, the need to manually stitch together exports, and the inability to surface insights without external tools. Impacted roles: Admins, Marketers, Leadership.

"Reporting should be a one-stop shop. I shouldn't need to open multiple messages to find results."

— Participant #14

"We just paid $800 for a custom report telling us who opted into notifications — why isn't that included?"

— Participant #28

Theme 2 — Permissions & Admin Roles Create Bottlenecks

Many teams only granted Super Admin access to a few users due to risk concerns — but this blocked critical tasks like reporting, template editing, and data exports. Users noted inconsistency in permissions across modules. Impacted roles: Community Managers, Content Editors.

"Too many features require Super Admin access. It creates a bottleneck for our team."

— Participant #66

"As a Community Admin, I still can't edit email templates. Why not?"

— Participant #64

Theme 3 — Outdated, Non-Intuitive UI

The interface was frequently described as dated, clunky, and difficult to navigate — especially compared to competitors like HubSpot or Mailchimp. Sentiment was consistent across user types and tenure levels. Affected areas: Navigation, Email Builder, Campaign Setup.

"Menu structure is a maze. I've been using it for months and still get lost."

— Participant #12

"It doesn't look modern — especially when compared to HubSpot or Mailchimp."

— Participant #41

Theme 4 — Email Functionality Causes Drop-Off

Email formatting, list management, and unsubscribe controls were seen as incomplete or non-customizable. Given the centrality of email to community and campaign workflows, these issues had an outsized impact on overall platform satisfaction. Affected areas: Templates, Delivery, List Management.

"We can't customize the unsubscribe page, which leads to users dropping off all communications."

— Participant #5

"The email builder is slow, and formatting keeps breaking when copied from templates."

— Participant #56

Bright Spot: Support — Across all score tiers, the Support team was the single most consistently praised element of the Thrive experience. "Ask the Advisor" received multiple unsolicited endorsements. This was a genuine trust asset — and a strategic entry point for onboarding and adoption programs. "Ask the Advisor has been a game-changer." — Participant #71

Participant Personas — Thrive Study

Who Was Using Thrive, and How They Experienced It

Four consistent user archetypes emerged from qualitative coding, each experiencing friction in distinct but often overlapping ways.

  • Super Admins

    Role restrictions force all tasks to funnel through them. Can't delegate to Community Admins. Constant bottlenecking from ticketing workflows.

    "I'm the only one who can do certain things — everyone funnels requests to me."

  • Marketers

    Email formatting inflexibility, reporting too fragmented to be useful, confusing unsubscribe workflow limiting control over member communications.

    "I just want to run a campaign and measure it end-to-end in one place."

  • End Users

    Confusing navigation, dated interface, difficulty locating content or understanding what the platform offers them.

    "It looks dated, and I can't always find what I'm looking for."

  • Implementers

    AMS/CRM integration complexity, inconsistent setup guidance, support knowledge gaps across connected systems.

    "The build was hard, but we're in a much better place now."

Recommendations — Thrive Study

What I Recommended

Five prioritized recommendations delivered to the product team in June 2025, based on theme frequency, participant impact, and business implications.

  1. Unify Reporting

    Consolidate message-level and platform-level analytics into a single interface. Directly addresses the most cited pain point across all user types — reduces staff time, improves visibility into outcomes.

  2. Expand Permissions Granularity

    Implement more granular role-based permissions so Community Admins can handle routine tasks without Super Admin escalation. Empowers teams and significantly reduces support burden.

  3. Modernize Navigation

    Overhaul menu structure and layout for discoverability. Consistent sidebar navigation, pinned content, and smart filtering were the most-requested specific improvements across all user types.

  4. Improve Email Template Flexibility

    Address formatting breakage, slow email builder load times, and inability to customize unsubscribe flows — issues that disproportionately impacted the lowest-scoring participants.

  5. Surface Support Resources Earlier

    Promote "Ask the Advisor" proactively during onboarding — before users hit frustration points. Leverage the Support team's strong reputation as a trust-building and retention tool.

Impact

From Data to Decisions

What the Research Changed

  • Established Higher Logic's first-ever systematic usability baseline across its product portfolio — replacing anecdote-driven quality conversations with a shared, defensible measurement framework
  • RM and IZ findings directly informed which marketing automation features to preserve, consolidate, or rebuild — turning qualitative complaints into prioritized strategic roadmap input
  • Findings synthesized into 12 prioritized product recommendations, of which leadership approved and implemented 7 — translating research directly into roadmap decisions
  • Delivered quick-win improvements from the Informz study that the engineering team could ship without waiting for a full planning cycle
  • Built an at-risk participant pipeline: F-scoring respondents flagged by name, organization, and score and routed to their CSM for proactive outreach — turning research data into a churn-prevention tool
  • Closed the feedback loop with Informz participants, demonstrating that research participation produced visible outcomes and building a durable, engaged panel for future studies
  • Placed the flagship platform in the 15th percentile for global usability ahead of a planned AI rollout — a data-backed argument for fixing foundational experience before adding new complexity
  • Proved usability debt was systemic across all three products — reframing quality from isolated complaints into a cross-product strategic priority
Reflection

What I Learned

What worked well

  • Mixed-methods design gave quantitative credibility and qualitative depth in the same deliverable
  • Consistent SUS methodology enabled true cross-product and competitive comparison
  • Multi-channel recruitment for Informz significantly improved sample quality and representativeness
  • Thematic coding created reusable frameworks — each study built on the last
  • At-risk flagging created direct business value beyond the research itself
  • Closing the feedback loop built a durable, engaged participant panel

What I'd do differently

  • Align with stakeholders upfront on what score thresholds would trigger which decisions — before fielding, not after
  • Run studies on a fixed cadence from the start to enable longitudinal tracking
  • Segment scores by user tenure and role from day one rather than as post-hoc analysis
  • Build a lighter-weight quarterly pulse survey for ongoing tracking between full studies
