How I got here

I started in management consulting at IBM, then moved to Ignyte Group, a boutique consulting agency, where my only client was Washington, DC's transit agency. That's where I found the part of the work I loved most: sitting with people, figuring out what was really going wrong, and making it clear enough to fix.

In 2020, I went through General Assembly's UX Immersive. In 2021, I joined Higher Logic as its first UX researcher. After four years, a company-wide restructuring ended my role. Since then, I've kept building, including Spoon Tracker, an app I designed and built for myself, and this site.

How I work

I live with a neurological disease. It has made me far more conscious of everything around me, and I understand people at a deeper level because of it. The grief and loss that come with it, almost daily, have made me both more empathetic and more analytical, and more confident in figuring out what I need. I'm proud of it, and of how much more confident it's made me over the past five years. That carries straight into my work.

Some of my favorite moments in research start with "Wow, that's a great question." When someone stops to think, they share more, and that's where the real gold usually is. I give them room. Sometimes it's a small joke to lighten the mood, sometimes it's sitting in the silence, and sometimes it's "Thank you, I was hoping that'd be a good one."

When it's time to share what I found, I keep it quick and concise, then make sure the people affected are taken care of. And I don't think AI is a cure-all. Not everyone needs or wants it, and you have to design for that too.

Outside of work

My happy place is at a concert. The genre doesn't matter as much as the people on stage. I love seeing music performed by people who can't imagine doing anything else with their lives. Their joy brings me joy.

I love all forms of yoga, but yin is my favorite. It's totally accessible, and it gives me a deep, instant connection to my mind and body.

And I travel to as many new parts of the USA as I can, and back to London, where I lived as a kid.
